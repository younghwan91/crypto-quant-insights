Adding 30 Symbols Doubled the Return. Drawdown Moved 0.3pp.

The assumption that "more symbols = more risk" isn't the whole picture. The relevant variable is the filter selectivity applied to those symbols — not the universe size itself.

We tested four universe sizes on the same strategy over the same 6-year period: 20, 30, 40, and 50 symbols. The filter kept strict eligibility criteria unchanged across all four runs — the same volume, volatility, and trend conditions were required to take a position.

The result: returns scaled approximately linearly with universe size (r² = 0.94 across the four sizes). Max drawdown was statistically indistinguishable across configurations — moving less than 1 percentage point between 20 and 50 symbols. Trade count scaled as expected.

The mechanism: when a strict filter is applied, expanding the universe doesn't lower the bar — it gives the same high bar more candidates. The marginal symbol added at position 31 has to pass the same eligibility check as symbol 1. The pool of qualified trades grows, but average trade quality (measured as avg PnL per position) improved from 1.21% to 1.34% despite the larger raw candidate pool. Filters in high-turnover markets are consistently finding the highest-quality setups among the new arrivals.

The correlation structure also helps. During the 6-year window, the median pairwise correlation across symbols in our universe sat at 0.23 in normal conditions. Adding more diverse symbols doesn't uniformly increase portfolio correlation — it depends on whether the signals trigger across similar market conditions on similar symbols simultaneously.

One important caveat: this result assumes the filter is strict enough that not every symbol passes in every period. A looser filter on a larger universe produces a different outcome — more positions, correlated, all at once.

같은 전략, 같은 6년, 종목 수만 20→30→40→50으로 바꿨다. 필터 기준은 고정했다 — 거래량, 변동성, 추세 조건이 동일하게 요구됐다.
