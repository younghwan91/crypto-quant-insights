We screened 132 parameter configurations across 6 years of Binance USDT-M perpetuals. The filter for "structural" candidates: PF > 1.0, trade count above 1,000, and no losing years. 68 of 132 passed on PF and trade count. On losing years: 1 configuration out of 132 passed all three — zero losing years across 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025.

That configuration's worst year was 2023: +50.1%. Its best year was 2020: +517.9%. Across the full 6-year period, max drawdown stayed below 32.4% in every year individually. Calmar ratio across the full period: 5.8.

The mechanism worth understanding: the yearly constraint eliminates configurations that are simply well-calibrated to one or two strong years and underperform in the rest. 2022 and 2023 are the filters that matter most here — 2022 included Luna and FTX (sharp drawdowns testing risk controls), 2023 was a low-momentum environment that tested whether the edge survived without a bull market tailwind. Configurations that relied on trending conditions through 2021 typically failed 2023.

The second-ranked configuration by our Calmar-weighted aggression score had one losing year. Its 6-year cumulative return was similar, but its worst year was -2.8% (2021). The presence of one losing year doesn't make it an invalid strategy — but it does change the drawdown sequencing risk.

The critical point is methodological: if you rank configurations by full-period return alone, the ranking ignores how concentrated those returns were. A 6-year Calmar of 5.8 with zero losing years is a structurally different result from 5.8 Calmar that required one exceptional year to hold up.

Caveat: 6 years of data includes the most exceptional bull market in crypto history (2020-2021). Zero-losing-year performance over this window doesn't guarantee similar consistency in future regimes. The OOS question remains open.
