Your In-Sample PF Was 2.16. Your Out-of-Sample PF Was 1.38.

That 36% drop is the number that matters for live trading. The 2.16 is the number that gets put in pitch decks.

We ran a strict IS/OOS validation across 7 parameter variants on 60 USDT-M perpetuals. The split: 2020-2022 as in-sample (2.9 years), 2023-2025 as out-of-sample (3.1 years). All variants were optimized on IS data only, then evaluated on OOS without modification.

IS profit factors across variants: 2.16 to 3.51.
OOS profit factors: 1.38 to 1.58.

Every single variant failed the OOS/IS ratio threshold of 0.85. The range was 0.45 to 0.64. In plain terms: OOS performance was 36% to 55% below IS, regardless of which configuration we tested.

The mechanism is specific to this dataset. The 2020-2022 window included the COVID crash recovery, a sustained bull market, the China mining ban, Luna, and FTX — a full boom-and-bust cycle that generated exceptional directional edge for momentum-based signals. The 2023-2025 window is a market normalization period: sustained trends are shorter, mean-reversion competes with momentum, and the structural tailwinds from 2021 are absent.

A model calibrated on 2020-2022 is partially calibrated on extraordinary conditions. Its IS PF contains that windfall. The OOS PF doesn't.

The practical implication is not that OOS PF 1.38 is bad — it isn't. A consistent 1.38 over 3.1 years of real data, including period 2023-2025, is a meaningful result. The implication is that presenting the IS number as the expected live number requires a disclosure: it spans the most favorable directional period in crypto history.

One structural check worth adding: if your IS period includes 2021, decompose how much of your total IS return came from that single year. If it exceeds 40%, your IS is partially a 2021 proxy.

2020~2022에 최적화된 모델은 부분적으로 특별한 시기에 맞춰진 것이다. IS PF에는 그 기간의 이익이 포함돼 있다. OOS PF에는 없다.
